Russia-Ukraine war: US-brokered peace talks

⌄

Coverage of the US-brokered shuttle diplomacy - Witkoff and Kushner's meetings with Putin and Zelenskyy - reveals distinct editorial angles. Die Welt offers the most neutral, factual account, relaying Moscow's description of the talks as "extremely honest" alongside continued drone strikes and General Hodges' skeptical commentary on US resolve, without taking a firm stance itself. The Guardian provides the fullest on-the-ground picture across its pieces: one traces the envoys' train journey to Kyiv and Zelenskyy's hopes for a "winter package" and expanded trilateral talks with Europe, another highlights deep Ukrainian distrust of both Russian intentions and Trump's personal commitment, and a third features an internal Ukrainian voice - defence adviser Serhii Beskrestnov - warning that the war has become a mutually destructive stalemate nobody can win, with a looming winter energy crisis. Süddeutsche Zeitung takes the most critical line, running two pieces that portray Trump's envoys as treating diplomacy like a business deal, naive and overly sympathetic to Putin, arguing that without real pressure on Moscow genuine peace remains unlikely. The Daily Telegraph strikes a markedly different note, arguing not on general moral grounds but from strategic calculation: with Putin weakened economically and militarily, now is precisely the wrong moment to ease pressure or accept a deal that lets Russia keep occupied territory - cutting against the momentum toward a settlement implied by other outlets.
