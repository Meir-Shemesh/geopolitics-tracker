Trade & Economics

US-Canada Tariff War

All four papers cover the breakdown of US-Canada trade talks and Carney's dollar-for-dollar retaliation. The Daily Telegraph runs the story from three angles: a news report on Trump's '51st state' rhetoric, an opinion piece cheering Carney's toughness and urging Britain to stand up to Brussels similarly, and an editorial calling the tariff war 'madness' that damages Western unity against rivals like China. The Guardian offers a news-analysis account of the escalation and documents criticism from US lawmakers of Trump's tariffs. Die Welt reports the clash factually, juxtaposing Carney's and Trump's competing claims. Süddeutsche Zeitung takes the clearest stance, with commentary praising Carney's firm, clear response as a model contrasted favorably with Europe's more conciliatory approach to Trump, alongside a separate report on business anxieties over rising costs and disrupted supply chains.

Sources: The Daily Telegraph, Die Welt, The Guardian, Süddeutsche Zeitung

Security & Conflict

UK Helps Ukraine Build Long-Range Missiles

Both The Daily Telegraph and The Guardian report the UK Prime Minister's pledge to give Ukraine the blueprints to build its own Storm Shadow long-range missiles, citing the same vow that 'Russia should be in no doubt of our resolve.' Both frame the move as a resolute, unwavering show of support, with The Daily Telegraph emphasizing the strategic significance of sharing military know-how while The Guardian frames it more as a shared moral commitment against Russian aggression.

Sources: The Daily Telegraph, The Guardian

Security & Conflict

Russian Hybrid Warfare Against NATO and Europe

This theme receives extensive coverage across three papers. The Daily Telegraph frames escalating Russian sabotage, cyberattacks and assassination attempts as a test of NATO resolve calibrated to stay below the Article 5 threshold, and separately covers Iceland's struggle to counter Russian and Chinese activity in the Arctic without a military of its own. Die Welt runs commentary describing NATO as fighting an 'unequal battle' against hybrid tactics that stay below the threshold for a unified response, warning of the risk of uncontrolled escalation. Süddeutsche Zeitung offers the German domestic angle directly: one piece calls on Berlin to respond decisively to Putin, while another counsels calm composure as the wiser response to Russia's ambiguous, low-level provocations, while acknowledging the risk of further escalation.

Sources: The Daily Telegraph, Die Welt, Süddeutsche Zeitung

Security & Conflict

Ukraine War: Diplomacy, Elections and Strikes

Die Welt devotes several pieces to US diplomacy on the war: an analysis contrasting a skeptical reading of the Witkoff-Kushner Kyiv visit as mere symbolic PR with a more optimistic view of shifting US sentiment; a piece arguing that without US pressure Putin will not budge; and a report on Putin's threat of harsher retaliation for Ukraine's economic strikes. The Guardian, by contrast, focuses on Zelenskyy's argument against holding wartime elections, and on sabotage inside Crimea and Russia — including a suspected assassination of a Russian naval officer and a profile of an alleged Russian saboteur recruited by Kyiv — while carefully flagging the unverifiable nature of Russian claims.

Sources: Die Welt, The Guardian

Migration & Society

Germany's Migration Policy: Syrians and Dublin Cases

Die Welt and Süddeutsche Zeitung approach German migration policy from different angles: Die Welt argues for limiting Syrian refugees' right to stay to those economically integrated and paying taxes, and separately attacks a center-right politician for a fatalistic 'surrender' narrative about mass migration's irreversibility. Süddeutsche Zeitung, by contrast, takes an opposite, legally grounded critical stance, arguing that Germany's complete stripping of benefits from asylum seekers in Dublin cases violates EU law and must stop.

Sources: Die Welt, Süddeutsche Zeitung

Domestic Politics

The Rise of the AfD in Germany

This topic receives broad, varied treatment across the German-language press. The Daily Telegraph reports on the AfD's plan in Saxony-Anhalt to segregate special-needs and refugee children from mainstream schooling. Die Welt offers a contrasting angle, attributing the party's rise to contemptuous Western German elite attitudes toward the East. Süddeutsche Zeitung publishes the most extensive analysis: a piece on the AfD's instrumentalization of Bauhaus culture and heritage as a hegemonic tool, a critique of a cultural manifesto meant to defend the Bauhaus that fails to grapple with its complicated history, an argument that AfD policies would economically harm Germany, and a book review of a dystopian 2033 AfD-government scenario examining the affect-driven politics fueling the party's rise.

Sources: The Daily Telegraph, Die Welt, Süddeutsche Zeitung

Energy & Environment

North Sea and European Energy Policy

The Daily Telegraph and The Guardian sharply diverge on UK energy policy: The Daily Telegraph gives voice to an oil executive who argues punitive taxation and regulatory unpredictability have made Britain 'uninvestable.' The Guardian, in two pieces, argues the opposite — that North Sea oil has long since lost its economic significance and that its persistence as a nostalgic political symbol (exploited by Farage, Badenoch and Trump) distracts from the real solution of publicly or community-owned renewable energy, as in Norway. Süddeutsche Zeitung adds two European angles: criticism of Germany's insufficient gas storage preparation before winter, and an argument that this summer's heatwaves exposed nuclear power in France as just as climate-vulnerable as renewables.

Sources: The Daily Telegraph, The Guardian, Süddeutsche Zeitung
