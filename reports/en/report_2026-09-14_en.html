AI regulation debate and US-China tech rivalry

⌄

All the outlets engage with the wave of calls by AI industry leaders like Anthropic's Dario Amodei to slow development, but they diverge sharply on how sincere those calls are. The Daily Telegraph is the most openly cynical, framing the push as regulatory capture designed to hobble open-source rivals and China while serving Anthropic's commercial interests ahead of its IPO, hinting that "safety" rhetoric masks softening demand for costly data-centre investment. The Guardian takes a similarly skeptical view but widens the lens to the clashing positions of Amodei, Musk and Altman, stressing that experts see these proposals as inadequate or even counterproductive, against the backdrop of US-China rivalry. Süddeutsche Zeitung devotes the most coverage to the topic and spans a range of stances: questioning whether safety warnings are clever marketing, but also taking seriously internal researcher warnings and security incidents as evidence that companies themselves cannot control what they've built - prompting them to beg government for oversight, which the paper calls a stark case of state failure. It also separately criticizes the Trump administration, arguing its AI policy is driven mainly by the desire to beat China rather than genuine safety concern. Die Welt strikes a more earnest, pro-regulation tone, reporting favorably on proposals for international AI governance modeled on Cold War arms-control treaties, and in a separate piece highlights Europe's geopolitical vulnerability from dependence on US and Chinese AI providers, calling for European technological sovereignty. The Washington Post contributes two distinct practical angles: one draws a legal parallel between AI leaders' existential-risk rhetoric and Sam Bankman-Fried's reckless utilitarianism, warning such statements could become liability risks; the other dismisses international treaties as unrealistic and instead proposes physically restricting AI's access to weapons of mass destruction - a pragmatic stance that sidesteps the debate over motive.

⌄
