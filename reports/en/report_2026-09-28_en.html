NATO/Europe vs Russian hybrid warfare and escalation fears

⌄

All three articles agree that Russia is intensifying hybrid warfare against NATO and Europe, but each frames the stakes differently. The Daily Telegraph, in Emily Smith's report, foregrounds the operational and dramatic dimension, describing Baltic states as alarmed enough to be drawing up evacuation plans for civilians in case of a Russian attack, on the premise that a weakened, battle-worn Putin is likely to test NATO's resolve through provocation and sabotage. Die Welt, in a piece by Lara Jäkel and Carolina Drüten, takes a more analytical approach, tracing how Russia is taking greater risks to gain leverage in the Ukraine war, and highlighting that a European wargame exercise exposed the continent's inability to formulate a coherent "endgame" strategy for responding to such escalation. USA Today, reported by Francesca Chambers and Miles J. Herszenhorn, widens the lens to the transatlantic policy debate, emphasizing the tension between the need for coordinated US-European responses - public attribution of attacks, continued sanctions and deterrence - and a deliberate refusal to resort to terrorist-style retaliation or direct war with Russia, as captured in an official's insistence that Western countries "are not terrorist countries." Thus, while the Daily Telegraph stresses local anxiety and ground-level emergency preparation, Die Welt points to a broader European strategic deficit, and USA Today frames the issue as a policy dilemma balancing deterrence against restraint.
