US sanctions and Iran's economic crisis

⌄

Five outlets weigh the effectiveness of economic pressure on Iran, arriving at markedly different conclusions. The Guardian, across two pieces, takes the most critical stance toward Washington: one article argues the new sanctions are almost superfluous given Iran's economy is already collapsing, accusing Tehran's leadership of cynically framing hardship as patriotic wartime sacrifice while concealing poverty statistics; the other turns its critical lens inward, portraying US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent as out of his depth in both the national debt crisis and managing the Iran sanctions campaign. Los Angeles Times offers a more analytical take, arguing Trump's combined sanctions-and-military strategy has failed to change Iranian behavior, leaving both sides locked in a stalemate driven by domestic politics on each side, with risks of further escalation. USA Today, by contrast, presents the most optimistic picture from Washington's perspective, reporting that sanctions and the blockade on oil exports are genuinely squeezing Iran's economy and becoming harder to withstand - though this could push Iran toward military escalation instead. The Wall Street Journal provides the broadest historical framing, contending that sanctions rarely succeed in forcing autocratic regimes to change course - citing Cuba, North Korea, Venezuela, Russia and Syria - and concluding that the pressure campaign against Iran likely faces similarly long odds. The result is a spectrum: from The Guardian's charge of cynicism on both the American and Iranian sides, through the analytical skepticism of Los Angeles Times and The Wall Street Journal, to USA Today's more cautiously optimistic read on the sanctions' bite.
