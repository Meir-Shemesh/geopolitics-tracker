Russia's winter strategy against Ukraine's resilience

⌄

Most of the coverage agrees on the underlying facts - Russia is intensifying strikes on energy infrastructure, bridges and cities as winter approaches - but the sources diverge sharply on motive and likely outcome. Die Welt offers the bleakest framing across two pieces: one report, drawing on intelligence and a New York Times account, warns that Russia is planning a massive missile assault on energy infrastructure that could force many Ukrainians to relocate; a companion piece quotes military analyst Mykola Bieleskow arguing that the real aim is to render cities uninhabitable in order to erode Ukrainian resilience and squeeze Western allies into providing more financial aid, with his pointed observation that Ukraine is "ultimately fighting this war alone." The Washington Post, in Francesca Ebel's reporting, strikes a similar note but focuses specifically on Kyiv, describing bridge strikes as a deliberate tactic to sow chaos, displace civilians and make the capital unlivable before winter. Against this, in his Guardian column Rajan Menon pushes back forcefully on the premise that the strategy can succeed, arguing that historical precedent shows bombing campaigns harden rather than break civilian morale, that Ukrainians overwhelmingly reject land-for-peace deals, and that Russia itself is straining under serious military and economic pressure. Süddeutsche Zeitung, through Frank Nienhuysen, takes a markedly different angle, highlighting not urban bombardment but a reciprocal drone war that is paralyzing both countries' airspace - quoting Zelensky's claim that Russian airspace itself is becoming entirely unsafe, a framing that emphasizes Ukrainian offensive capability rather than pure victimhood.
