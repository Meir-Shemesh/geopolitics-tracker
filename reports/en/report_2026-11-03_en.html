Die Welt, in Antonia Langford's report, offers a wholly different angle, setting aside both the cyberattack and the military violations to document the human toll: testimony from residents evacuated from occupied Oleshky describing starvation, destroyed homes and abuse by Russian soldiers during the long occupation.

Poland's constitutional court crisis

⌄

Coverage of Poland's constitutional court crisis spans Polish self-criticism, cautious support for Tusk, analytical warning, and factual reporting. Frankfurter Allgemeine Zeitung relays the view of the Polish paper Rzeczpospolita, which calls the crisis a "disgrace" for Poland's entire political class, blaming leaders for failing to deliver the state-oriented, bipartisan solution promised by the Tusk coalition back in 2023. The Guardian takes a more explicitly sympathetic line in its editorial, arguing that Donald Tusk's forceful move against the judicial obstruction orchestrated by PiS-backed President Nawrocki is legally and morally justified, even as it carries political risk and could embolden Europe's far right ahead of elections in Poland, France and Spain. In his column for Neue Zürcher Zeitung, Markus Ackeret strikes a more critical note toward Tusk's strategy, arguing that his "liberating blow" is politically understandable but costly, since it deepens polarization, unifies the PiS-led opposition, and fails to restore genuine legitimacy to the constitutional court. Alongside these two more interpretive takes, Süddeutsche Zeitung's report, bylined by Viktoria Großmann, offers a comparatively factual account of the escalating institutional conflict - involving the president, rival judges and competing assemblies - noting the involvement of EU and Council of Europe bodies in assessing the legitimacy of the disputed judges, and framing it as the sharpest clash over the court since the nationalist PiS government began plunging Poland's judiciary into chaos in 2015.
