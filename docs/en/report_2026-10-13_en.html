Trump's diesel deal and energy ceasefire in Russia-Ukraine war

⌄

Coverage of Trump's Russia diesel deal and the declared "energy ceasefire" splits along several lines. Die Welt runs two pieces arguing the truce is effectively one-sided: Russia keeps bombing Ukrainian energy infrastructure while the deal's real purpose, the paper suggests, is to stop Ukrainian strikes on Russian refineries that Trump blames for high US diesel prices; a second piece argues Moscow's silence is strategically rational since it already won concessions without paying a political price, insisting it wants a full settlement rather than a pause that only helps Ukraine's military. Frankfurter Allgemeine Zeitung, Süddeutsche Zeitung, and The Washington Post all report on sharp bipartisan congressional criticism calling the deal an illegal violation of 2022 sanctions law that throws Putin a financial lifeline while he keeps killing Ukrainian civilians. Neue Zürcher Zeitung carries two critical columns: one (bylined "cbs.") argues Trump is far more cautious than the EU on sanctions, prioritizing cheaper gas before the midterms over tougher measures, while Christian Weisflog's column calls the deal "senseless," likening it to "almost a reward" for Putin's war crimes. A companion piece in Frankfurter Allgemeine Zeitung, by Christian Siedenbiedel, adds a market-focused angle, noting the deal's price effect was brief given analysts' doubts Russia can even deliver the promised volumes. The Los Angeles Times focuses on the political victory this hands the Kremlin, straining Western unity and Zelensky's negotiating leverage, while The Washington Post's news report by Ellen Francis and colleagues exposes a split within US policy itself, between NATO's commander publicly vowing to repel Russia and US officials privately pressuring Kyiv to halt refinery strikes. The Washington Post's editorial board directly attacks the logic of pressuring the victim rather than the aggressor, and columnist Max Boot extends this into a broader critique of Trump's failed "dollar diplomacy" with authoritarian regimes. Finally, three separate Washington Post investigative pieces - one bylined by Evan Hill, Catherine Belton and Jarrett Ley, and two further unsigned investigations - provide detailed factual background on how Ukraine's precision drone campaign has crippled Russian refining capacity and triggered the country's worst fuel crisis since the Soviet collapse, context that helps explain why Trump is so keen to stop these strikes.

NATO defense posture against Russia

⌄

Several sources converge on the same core event - NATO Supreme Allied Commander Europe Grynkewich's unveiling of the alliance's new defense doctrine - but frame it differently. Frankfurter Allgemeine Zeitung foregrounds the blunt military message that NATO will "smash" any attack, presenting it as an unprecedentedly explicit deterrent statement while stressing the posture remains defensive.
