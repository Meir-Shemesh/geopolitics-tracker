Security & Conflict

Western aid to Ukraine and the air-defence gap

The Daily Telegraph, Süddeutsche Zeitung, and Die Welt all cover fresh Western aid pledges to Ukraine, but The Daily Telegraph is most pointed in arguing that Andy Burnham's Storm Shadow gift, however symbolically bold, misses Kyiv's real need - Patriot interceptors only America can supply - while a companion piece praises Britain's resolve. Süddeutsche Zeitung frames continued aid supportively while also calling for tougher sanctions, and Die Welt likewise flags the persistent Patriot shortfall while stressing that Putin shows no genuine interest in ending the war. All three broadly endorse continued support, differing mainly in emphasis between hardware gaps, sanctions, and Russian intent.

Sources: The Daily Telegraph, Die Welt, Süddeutsche Zeitung

Security & Conflict

Genocide or self-defence: the framing of Gaza

Gaza coverage splits sharply along source lines: The Daily Telegraph runs an opinion piece explicitly rejecting genocide accusations against Israel, citing Gaza's population growth and Israel's stated aim of defeating Hamas rather than annihilating a people. The Guardian, by contrast, covers a Venice-premiering documentary alleging a calculated system of mass killing of civilians, and separately reports on forensic audio work suggesting Israeli forces directly targeted aid workers - both Guardian pieces converge on an accusatory framing. Süddeutsche Zeitung takes yet another angle, arguing that Netanyahu's long tenure has brutalized Israeli society and made peace politically taboo.

Sources: The Daily Telegraph, The Guardian, Süddeutsche Zeitung

Trade & Economics

The US-Canada trade war

This story receives extensive, varied treatment across all outlets. The Daily Telegraph runs two opinion pieces - one warning Canada's economy will ultimately suffer most, another likening the trade rupture to a costly overreach the White House will regret amid American war-weariness. Die Welt reconstructs in detail how the near-finished deal collapsed at the last minute amid internal Trump administration power struggles, while Süddeutsche Zeitung focuses on the mutual damage to two deeply intertwined economies, calling the breakdown a needless political gamble ahead of midterms. The Guardian is most sympathetic to Canada: two opinion pieces praise Carney's refusal to be bullied, a news piece frames the talks' collapse as a warning to the world, and another highlights the fight over Quebec's French-language protections as the specific flashpoint.

Sources: The Daily Telegraph, Die Welt, The Guardian, Süddeutsche Zeitung

Domestic Politics

The rise of the AfD in Germany

Die Welt and Süddeutsche Zeitung both extensively cover the AfD's rise but from different vantage points. Die Welt runs a cluster of pieces: one arguing the surge stems from genuine migration and deportation policy failures rather than mere sentiment; another defending Sachsen-Anhalt voters as rationally responding to establishment failure rather than acting foolishly; and two pieces reviewing Justus Bender's speculative novel about a future AfD government, warning - somewhat wryly - about the erosion of the political 'firewall.' Süddeutsche Zeitung takes a more critical tack throughout: one piece attacks AfD leader Chrupalla for climate-change denial, another profiles an activist trying to reclaim national symbols co-opted by the AfD, a social psychologist warns of the normalization of right-wing extremism, and a methodological piece cautions against misleading historical analogies comparing the AfD's rise to past political shifts.

Sources: Die Welt, Süddeutsche Zeitung
