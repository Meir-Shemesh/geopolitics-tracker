⌄

EU-China trade dispute over electric vehicles and tariffs

⌄

Coverage of the EU-China EV trade dispute splits between institutional reporting that endorses a hardening European stance and more critical or industry-focused angles. Frankfurter Allgemeine Zeitung runs several pieces converging on the same message: a translated commentary from the Belgian paper De Tijd argues the deal limiting Chinese hybrid imports proves the EU can wield its economic weight effectively against Beijing, a separate report covers a Franco-German push to extend trade-defense tools to chemicals and machinery, and a multi-author feature traces how Chinese automakers like BYD, Geely and SAIC are expanding globally - together painting a picture of a confidently toughening EU. Die Welt, by contrast, foregrounds the industrial angle: a piece by Christoph Kapalschinski describes European carmakers racing to launch cheap small EVs in response to Chinese competition, while another article voices criticism from a Chery representative who argues that shielding the European market betrays its tradition of openness and will hurt consumers through higher prices. The Guardian adds the British angle, reporting that the UK is weighing alignment with EU tariffs on Chinese EVs, alongside China's warning that such a move would be discriminatory and violate WTO rules. Los Angeles Times, through Chester Dawson, takes the most global, structural view, citing an industry report that argues China's cost and software advantages are fundamentally harder to replicate than past competitive shocks, prompting governments worldwide to restrict Chinese vehicle imports.

⌄
