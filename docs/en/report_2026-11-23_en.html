COP31 climate summit collapse in Antalya

⌄

Most outlets, including The Guardian, Los Angeles Times, Washington Post, USA Today, The Globe and Mail, Die Welt, Frankfurter Allgemeine Zeitung and Neue Zürcher Zeitung, report the same basic facts of COP31's collapse in Antalya: oil states led by Saudi Arabia and Russia blocked any mention of transitioning away from fossil fuels, the EU and allied nations rejected the proposed "Antalya pact" as greenwashing, and UN Secretary-General Guterres voiced deep disappointment. Beyond that shared factual base, clear differences in emphasis and tone emerge. The Guardian is the most pointed and critical voice: in a column, Fiona Harvey argues that refusing to discuss fossil fuels is no longer a tenable position, and in another piece co-written with Graham Readfearn she calls it "the worst Cop ever," accusing rich nations of hypocrisy for expanding their own fossil fuel production while vulnerable countries suffer. The Daily Telegraph offers two distinct angles: a straight news report frames the collapse as an EU-versus-petrostates clash, while columnist Matt Oliver interprets the failure as evidence of a new "age of the strongman," noting how the Trump-Xi-Putin summit (dubbed "Yalta 2.0") overshadowed the climate talks, and a further Telegraph piece cynically questions the point of these annual gatherings beyond "virtue signalling" and networking. Die Welt likewise combines a factual dpa report with a sharper opinion piece by Axel Bojanowski, who argues that the real "climate lie" is the pretense that viable alternatives already exist, urging investment in innovation rather than subsidies. At Frankfurter Allgemeine Zeitung, Hanna Decker attributes the breakdown partly to the hosts' diplomatic clumsiness and sees climate multilateralism shifting toward informal coalitions rather than disappearing outright. Süddeutsche Zeitung's Michael Bauchmüller calls the summit "a setback for the world" and calls for reforming and shrinking the COP process, while praising the EU and island states for standing firm. Neue Zürcher Zeitung notably pairs straight reporting on Switzerland's rejection of the pact with a column by Peter Rásonyi arguing the failure should not be overstated, since the clean-energy transition continues regardless, driven by economics rather than diplomacy. The Globe and Mail adds a distinct national angle, highlighting criticism that Canada was "missing in action" at the talks.
