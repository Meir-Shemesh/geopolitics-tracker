Domestic Politics

Zelensky's dismissal of Fedorov and Ukraine's governance crisis

The Guardian runs two pieces treating Fedorov's dismissal as a direct political challenge to Zelensky, with one report on the protests and one analysis arguing his corruption critique is credible while Ukrainian civil society still checks presidential power. Süddeutsche Zeitung frames Fedorov as a credible, popular rival demanding wartime elections despite constitutional bans, while flagging the risks involved. The Daily Telegraph strikes a notably different, twofold stance: one piece argues Zelensky's sidelining of rivals is a self-defeating blunder that weakens Ukrainian unity to Russia's benefit, while another separately insists that calling elections now would be dangerously destabilizing and play into Putin's hands.

Sources: The Daily Telegraph, The Guardian, Süddeutsche Zeitung

Security & Conflict

Russia-Ukraine war: civilian casualties and escalation rhetoric

Die Welt offers two angles: one rejects moral equivalence between the warring sides, arguing Russia deliberately targets civilians as a terror strategy while Ukraine's civilian casualties are largely collateral, and another dissects Patriarch Kirill's framing of the war as a sacralizing 'metaphysical' struggle. Süddeutsche Zeitung, in its report on the war's trajectory, argues Russia's nuclear escalation threats are largely bluffs meant to deter Western support, while Russia itself suffers real economic and military damage from Ukrainian drone strikes.

Sources: Die Welt, Süddeutsche Zeitung

Security & Conflict

NATO and Baltic security vis-à-vis Russia

Die Welt covers this in two pieces: one frames NATO's show of force near Kaliningrad as deliberate deterrence, warning Russia is likelier to wage covert hybrid warfare than direct attack; the other presents the Rail Baltica rail project as a strategic necessity to defend the vulnerable Suwalki Gap, warning against delay. Süddeutsche Zeitung, separately, focuses on mounting German political pressure to shut down Berlin's 'Russian House' over suspected espionage and disinformation, accusing the government of dangerous negligence.

Sources: Die Welt, Süddeutsche Zeitung

Süddeutsche Zeitung runs an interview with researcher Sylvia Sasse analyzing Russia's 'Matroshka' disinformation campaign as a radicalized continuation of old KGB tactics designed to split pro-Russian and critical voices and merge with global right-wing populist narratives.

Sources: Süddeutsche Zeitung

Migration & Society

Europe's migration crisis and asylum policy

This topic receives extensive treatment in Die Welt, including two interviews with Jan Solwyn arguing the Schengen/Dublin system has structurally collapsed and needs a centralized EU solution with detention facilities at external borders, a piece on Germany's tightening protections for Syrian refugees, and a critical article accusing German NGOs of undermining Italian border enforcement. The Daily Telegraph reports more journalistically on Spain's political backlash after sexual assaults in Ceuta exploited by right-wing parties, and on Germany's failure to deport 9,000 migrants, framing both as politically fraught for Merz's government. Süddeutsche Zeitung adds a different angle, criticizing Poland for avoiding honest debate on immigration despite economic need for foreign workers, contrasting it favorably with Spain's approach.

Sources: The Daily Telegraph, Die Welt, Süddeutsche Zeitung
