France nationwide riots and curfews

⌄

All the outlets cover the same core events - the mass weekend riots across France, more than 500,000 protesters, widespread looting, curfews in several cities, and the political storm over the interior minister's resignation offer - but frame them very differently. The Daily Telegraph casts the unrest as a broader warning to Europe, tying the breakdown of order to fears of a debt and fiscal crisis that could force ECB intervention, and blaming both far-left and far-right forces for exploiting the chaos. Die Welt, in Christophe Cornevin's reporting, stresses the state's intelligence failure - officials "flying blind" against a determined mass of rioters - while its translated piece from Le Figaro emphasizes exhausted police forces and a government turning to tougher judicial measures. Frankfurter Allgemeine Zeitung offers the widest range of voices: Michaela Wiegel argues in her column that the riots expose the failure of France's republican integration model for banlieue youth, while Marc Zitzmann documents the unexpected scale of violence and the ensuing left-right blame game; the paper also runs translated opinion pieces from The Independent and El Mundo calling for political dialogue over repression. Süddeutsche Zeitung provides particularly varied analysis: Alexandra Föderl-Schmid reports factually on the loss of control, Nils Minkmar sees the arson at the Limoges town hall as a dangerous new escalation tied to organized crime, and Cynthia Fleury argues that looting itself has become the goal rather than a protest for recognition as with the Gelets jaunes. Neue Zürcher Zeitung, through Christine Longin, highlights Macron's decision to travel abroad despite the crisis, while in a separate column Daniel Rickenbacher blames failed, dependency-creating integration spending alongside left-wing victimhood narratives. The Guardian layers several angles: Jon Henley reports on the curfews and separately argues Macron is a weakened, answerless president strengthening the far right; Angelique Chrisafis offers an on-the-ground account of Parisians' shock at scenes resembling "a war zone"; and another piece warns of an ominous political reckoning driven by Macron's austerity agenda. Los Angeles Times and The Globe and Mail focus mainly on factual accounts of police losing control and the blame-shifting between Macron's camp and Mélenchon's far left, while The Washington Post emphasizes the political fallout ahead of the presidential election and the expected boost to Marine Le Pen.
