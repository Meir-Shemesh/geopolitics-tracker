Biweekly Trends Report

September 17–30, 2026

The past two weeks were dominated by a broadening confrontation between Russia and the West: an intensifying drone/air war against Ukraine ran alongside rising Russian hybrid sabotage against NATO and Europe, while domestic pressure built on Russia's opposition ahead of Duma elections. In parallel, a cluster of flashpoints - Houthis/Red Sea tensions, the US-Iran standoff, and Venezuela - points to a broader pattern of US confrontation with Iran-aligned and adversarial actors across multiple regions simultaneously. Beyond this security thread, AI's risks and global regulatory competition, and turbulent German domestic politics (BSW, AfD), stood out as major but largely independent storylines rather than part of a single unifying narrative.

AI risk, regulation and the US-China race

14 of 14 days · 10 sources: Daily Telegraph, Der Spiegel, Die Welt, Economist, Guardian, Los Angeles Times, Süddeutsche Zeitung, USA Today, Wall Street Journal, Washington Post

Coverage shifted from framing AI as a dual threat - existential risk versus the danger of ceding ground to China - toward a growing, more cynical strand questioning the motives behind industry's own alarm. In mid-September, Die Welt and The Wall Street Journal warned of uncontrolled superintelligence while The Daily Telegraph urged Europe not to fall behind China and America. By September 30, Süddeutsche Zeitung's Gunnar Herrmann, Max Muth and Jannis Brühl argued that executives' doomsday warnings function as "regulatory capture," while USA Today's Dace Potas reached a similar conclusion from the opposite direction, viewing regulation calls as anti-competitive. Throughout, The Economist and Der Spiegel dissected structural failures of US-China cooperation, while The Wall Street Journal and The Daily Telegraph remained consistently most skeptical of any coordinated regulatory response.

German domestic politics: the AfD's rise and the mainstream's response

13 of 14 days · 10 sources: Daily Telegraph, Der Spiegel, Die Welt, Economist, Guardian, Los Angeles Times, Süddeutsche Zeitung, USA Today, Wall Street Journal, Washington Post

Coverage began as a sharp party-political clash - Die Welt entertaining arguments against isolating the AfD versus Süddeutsche Zeitung's uniform alarm - then, following the September 21-22 state election results, pivoted into crisis analysis of the CDU's collapse and Merz's weakened authority, with Schwesig's SPD campaign offered as a counter-model. Die Welt consistently carried the more varied, sometimes AfD-sympathetic or establishment-critical voices (Schneider on September 24, Florescu on September 30), while Süddeutsche Zeitung maintained a steadier critical posture toward the party throughout.

Russia's Hybrid War Against NATO and Europe

Coverage shifted from early, largely abstract warnings about a "coordinated campaign" demanding a tougher Western response, toward increasingly concrete, geographically specific incidents and sharper policy divisions by late September. The debate increasingly centered on how to deter Russia without resorting to terrorism or direct war.

Russia's drone and missile war on Ukraine

9 of 14 days · 8 sources: Daily Telegraph, Die Welt, Economist, Guardian, Los Angeles Times, Süddeutsche Zeitung, Wall Street Journal, Washington Post

Coverage began on September 18 as a single, largely unified analytical voice, almost entirely from The Economist, framing a "new phase of total war" with Russia pulling ahead technologically in drones and missiles. Over the following days the story broadened and fragmented: by September 30 it had grown to encompass economic costs (The Guardian citing $45 million-an-hour losses from air-raid alerts), human testimony (Süddeutsche Zeitung's Kyiv residents and Kherson accounts), and tactical-technological breakthroughs, including a September 28 Daily Telegraph interview with Ukraine's drone commander Robert Brovdi celebrating a shifted battlefield balance while warning of uncontrollable AI-driven drone escalation. The arc moves from a narrow strategic-technical framing toward a multi-layered portrait of attritional warfare hitting civilians, economies, and morale alike.
