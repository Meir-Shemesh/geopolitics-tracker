⌄

Coverage of the US midterms converges on Trump's weakened standing but diverges in emphasis. Global energy crisis and Middle East oil markets

⌄

The three sources identify strikingly different root causes for the ongoing energy turmoil. In The Daily Telegraph, Eir Nolsøe focuses on the Strait of Hormuz, warning that even though Gulf oil exports have recovered on paper, the underlying fragility and risk of renewed conflict could still produce a "terrifying" winter of high energy prices for Britain. Pjotr Sauer, writing in The Guardian, offers a markedly different diagnosis, arguing that the real driver of the diesel crunch is not Middle East tensions at all but Ukraine's sustained strikes on Russian oil refineries, a dynamic that is becoming a political liability for Trump ahead of the US midterms. Süddeutsche Zeitung's Roman-Emanuel Zgoll returns the focus to the Middle East, attributing fuel scarcity to the blockade of the Hormuz and Bab al-Mandab chokepoints and insisting that the crisis will persist as long as Trump fails to secure peace with Iran's regime - from which he draws a policy conclusion for Germany, urging reduced oil and gas dependence and faster expansion of renewables. The result is three distinct framings: British anxiety over Gulf-linked supply risk, a Russia-Ukraine-centered explanation for Western diesel shortages, and a German call for structural energy independence rooted in Middle Eastern instability.

Overall, the Telegraph's contribution stays closest to the investigative, intelligence-driven exposure of the specific plot, while Die Welt uses the same material as a springboard for a broader, more self-critical examination of German institutional unreadiness.
