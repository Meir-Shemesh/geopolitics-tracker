US war powers debate over the Iran war

⌄

All three pieces on this topic come from the Los Angeles Times, forming an internal chorus of views converging on the same underlying claim - that Congress has abdicated its constitutional role in war-making - though each takes a distinct angle. One opinion piece frames the failure as a structural, long-standing, bipartisan problem, calling for a bipartisan commission to rethink the balance of war powers between the president and Congress. A reader letter narrows the debate to a stricter constitutional argument, contending that the Iran war is illegal because only Congress has the power to declare war, and going further to argue that the War Powers Resolution itself is unconstitutional since it lets a president launch a war before securing congressional approval. A second letter takes a sharper political tone, directly accusing Republican lawmakers of cowardice and capitulation to Trump rather than engaging with the broader legal framework of war powers. Thus, while the first piece proposes institutional, cross-partisan reform, the two letters sharpen the critique into more immediate and pointed attacks - one grounded in constitutional principle, the other in partisan political blame - aimed squarely at the current administration.
